# Wiring the hobby-servo duck

Nothing here has been built or measured. Generated by `tools/make_docs.py` (tools/bom_hobby.py) from the spec, so the lead lengths are the model's.

## What is where

Everything electrical is in the body except the head's servos and the camera: the Pi Zero 2 W on 4 posts on the top plate, Adafruit's 16-Channel PWM / Servo Bonnet (a PCA9685, [its page](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3416), read 2026-11-06: 65 x 30.4 mm, 5-6 V servo supply) on the Pi's GPIO header, the Pi's 5 V buck converter and the IMU on the plate, the 2S pack under it, and a 6 V BEC in each pocket beside the hip yaw servos. So only the head's 3 servo leads, the head pitch servo's and the camera ribbon cross the neck.

The PCA9685 times the pulses in hardware (12-bit, set over I2C on GPIO 2 and 3), so the Pi's GPIO stays free and a busy Linux can't jitter a servo. One-way PWM: no position, load or temperature comes back from any servo. A walking policy that reads joint positions has to be fed the commands it sent, and the IMU is the only feedback. Nothing here has tried that.

## The servo leads: a star from the bonnet

PWM servos can't be chained: each has its own signal wire to its own channel. 15 joints need 15 of the 16 channels; channel 15 is spare. (14 channels, as asked, would be one servo short: the 15 are 10 in the legs, neck pitch, head pitch, head yaw, head roll and the beak. Drop the beak's servo to use 14.)

Each lead runs from where it leaves its servo (the far end face, low, through the window in its tray's end wall) along its own chain, joint by joint, to the bonnet. Lengths are measured on the model at the zero pose, with a 30 mm service loop at every joint it crosses, so the joint can turn its full range without pulling. Tie each loop to the bracket on each side of its joint, centred on the axis, with small cable ties (consumables in the BOM).

## Power

The leads' red and brown wires don't go through the bonnet: its V+ copper has no current rating on Adafruit's page, and a stalled leg would push 10 A through it. So power is a **shared 6 V / GND bus per side, with only the signal wires separate**:

- **Signal** (orange): each lead's signal pin, taken out of its JR housing, into a 1-pin housing on its channel's S pin on the bonnet.
- **V+ and GND** (red, brown): into a 2-pin housing per servo, on that side's bus: a 2-row header strip soldered to its BEC's output pigtail, in heat-shrink, taped in the BEC's pocket. Its mass is counted with the BEC.
- **The bonnet's V+ terminal stays empty.** Its GND is joined to the buses' GND by one wire, or the signals have no return. Its PCA9685's logic supply is expected from the Pi's header: confirm in Adafruit's guide for the bonnet before powering up.

|---|---|---|---|
| L 6 V BEC | the pack, 18 AWG pair | L hip yaw, L hip roll, L hip pitch, L knee, L ankle pitch, neck pitch, head pitch | 12.0 A |
| R 6 V BEC | the pack, 18 AWG pair | R hip yaw, R hip roll, R hip pitch, R knee, R ankle pitch, head yaw, head roll, beak | 10.2 A |
| Pi 5 V | USB buck converter, 2 wires from the pack | Pi Zero 2 W through GPIO pins 2 (5 V) and 6 (GND), the IMU from the Pi's 3.3 V | about 0.6 A (the Pi under load, a planning figure) |

Stall currents used: MG996R 1.4 A (TowerPro's page); DS3218 2.5 A (assumed: no maker figure found); MG90S 0.7 A (assumed: not on TowerPro's page).

**Sizing the BECs for the stall current.** Each BEC is sized for every servo on it stalling at once: about 12 A, so **a 6.0 V switching BEC, 2S input, rated 15 A peak and 8 A continuous or more**. In normal walking the torque checks put the servos at a third to two thirds of their holding torque, a few amps a side. The pockets are drawn at 39 x 21 x 6 mm, an 8 A UBEC's size from the hybrid's listing: a 15 A unit is likely bigger, so measure the one you buy and grow the pocket (spec/robot.py, `becs`). Set it to 6.0 V before connecting a servo: the MG996R is rated 4.8-6.6 V, and the torques here are 6 V figures.

**The pack.** All 15 stalling would draw about 22 A. The Zeee 2S 850 mAh 50C (as the hybrid's) allows 42 A by its label. No power switch: the XT30 is the disconnect, with the balance plug in the port at the back of the body.

## Signals and the neck

| What | From | To | Crosses the neck |
|---|---|---|---|
| I2C to the PCA9685 | Pi GPIO 2, 3 | the bonnet, on the same header | no |
| I2C to the IMU | the Pi's header pins 1 (3.3 V), 3 (SDA), 5 (SCL), 9 (GND), soldered under the header before the bonnet goes on | IMU on the top plate, 4 wires, 28 AWG | no |
| Servo leads | bonnet S pins and the buses | each servo, as the table above | head pitch, head yaw, head roll, beak |
| Camera ribbon | Pi's camera socket | camera on the back of the face | yes, through 4 joints: 414 mm with its loops. A Pi Zero ribbon that long, flexing at 4 joints, is the weakest link here: it is optional |

Check the I2C addresses don't clash: a PCA9685's address is between 0x40 and 0x7F, set by jumpers (Adafruit's page for the breakout, [product 815](https://www.adafruit.com/product/815)); the IMU's is on its own board's page.

## Not checked

- No current, voltage or lead length here is measured. The stall figures of the DS3218 and MG90S are assumptions.
- The service loops are modelled as length and mass only: no routed channel is drawn in the printed parts, past the window in each tray's end wall and the thigh plate's wire notches. Find where each loop sits on the first printed leg, and add a tie point there.
- Whether a one-way PWM duck can walk on a policy trained with joint feedback: untested.
